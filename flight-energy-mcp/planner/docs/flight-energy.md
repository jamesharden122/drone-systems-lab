# Measuring multirotor energy for a mission

Plan each interval with a duration in seconds and the mean electrical watts drawn from one battery. An airframe profile supplies that power from measurements of a comparable configuration and flight phase. Keep the raw logs and calibration record so another operator can tell which mission the estimate describes. The worked example uses synthetic measurements for all six phases. The benchmark uses Matrice 100 flight records. [Six-phase example](../examples/six_phase.rs) · [Real-flight benchmark](../benchmark/)

For profile lookup, use the same aircraft/equipment configuration, phase, workload and precipitation class as the calibration. Supply the same numeric factors as the profile, with each value inside its measured range (endpoints included). An omitted factor has no separately calibrated effect. Unknown precipitation describes an unreported condition in a retrospective record. Matching a dry or wet mission requires evidence with that precipitation class. Preserve the segment index and profile source when reporting coverage gaps and results.

## Measure power at a known boundary

For a whole-pack profile, log paired battery-terminal voltage and discharge current on the same clock. Record the pack identity, sensor location, calibration, units, discharge sign, sample rate and flight ID. A sum of ESC currents can miss the avionics branch. If the Jetson has its own battery, budget its energy against that battery.

For timestamped observations, form each power sample as `Pᵢ = Vᵢ × Iᵢ`. Integrate over elapsed seconds using the trapezoid rule, then divide the resulting watt-seconds by 3,600 for Wh. The phase mean is `3600 × phase_Wh / valid_duration_s`. Weight irregular samples by their intervals. Multiplying average voltage by average current loses their covariance during throttle changes. Align clocks, account for offset and drift, and inspect sensor update rates before interpreting fast transients. Split or exclude intervals across resets, dropouts, saturation or invalid telemetry. Record the excluded time and phase coverage.

For split-load profiles, record propulsion at the ESC input and each compute or auxiliary branch at a stated meter location. Each branch must represent a distinct load on the pack. A downstream electrical load contributes `load_W / converter_efficiency` at the pack, using measured efficiency at that load. A meter upstream of that converter already includes its losses. As a units check, a 9 W workload at 90% efficiency draws 10 W from the pack and consumes 1 Wh in six minutes. A whole-pack reading during the same workload already includes that 1 Wh.

## Describe the six phases

Use timestamped phase boundaries from the experiment and document the transition criteria. Assign each time interval to one phase. Treat time waiting in hover as a mission input. A route planner can supply that duration along with the expected travel times.

| Phase | Conditions to match in repeated observations |
|---|---|
| Takeoff | Start and end events, surface clearance, spool-up if it occurs within the recorded interval, takeoff mass |
| Climb | Signed vertical speed, altitude gain, local air density, any horizontal motion |
| Hover | Hold duration, payload position, wind and gusts, height above the surface |
| Cruise | Air-relative velocity, groundspeed along the leg, wind direction, payload and compute workload |
| Descent | Signed vertical speed, horizontal motion and air density. Use observed discharge for descent. |
| Landing | Start of final approach, touch-down and end event, near-ground maneuvering, any recorded spin-down |

## Account for operating conditions

**Ambient heat and altitude.** For dry air use local absolute pressure in Pa and temperature in K to estimate density in kg/m³ as `ρ = p / (287.05 × T)`. The planner's dry-air helper accepts ambient temperature in °C and converts it to K. Document humidity and the density method when humidity is significant. A height above the launch point measures climb distance and gives no direct local pressure. At fixed weight and rotor area, still-air momentum theory makes ideal induced hover power proportional to 1/√ρ. A 10% density reduction would raise this one power component by about 5.4%. Use matched electrical measurements to establish the change at the pack. Record climb power and climb duration separately. [NASA Glenn equation of state](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/equation-of-state/) · [Rodrigues et al., equation 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9403403/)

**Payload and center of gravity.** Record installed mass in kg, the body origin and axes, and CG offset in metres. Changing mass changes required hover thrust. Moving an equal mass can redistribute thrust between rotors and reduce the remaining control authority of an individual rotor. For a CG comparison, hold total mass fixed and record per-rotor loading, actuator saturation and tracking error along with pack watts. Energy margin measures remaining Wh. Stability and control headroom need their own observations. [Mueller and D’Andrea, section 6](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364915596233)

**Rotor efficiency, acceleration and stabilization.** Record propeller geometry, motor, ESC, supply voltage and controller configuration. At a comparable operating point, a thrust stand can measure thrust per input watt. Shaft power additionally requires torque and angular speed (`P = τω`, `ω = 2π × RPM / 60`). For transitions, synchronize per-rotor RPM, current and attitude records and compare integrated phase Wh across matched runs. For a rotor of inertia I, a speed increase from ω₁ to ω₂ adds ½I(ω₂² − ω₁²) joules of rotational energy. Aerodynamic work and drive losses also draw electrical energy during this interval. The whole-pack integral already captures the electrical cost of spin-up and stabilization. Use a measured discharge interval for spin-down. [Flight-validated electric powertrain measurements](https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/11/1/16)

**Wind and precipitation.** Compute air-relative velocity as ground velocity minus the velocity of the air, in the same coordinate frame. The planner's horizontal airspeed helper takes north/east ground and wind velocity components in m/s. Convert meteorological wind *from* directions to vectors pointing downwind first. At a fixed groundspeed a headwind changes airspeed. At a fixed airspeed it can extend the duration of a leg. Match gust exposure and use the caller's planned duration once. Record precipitation class, intensity if available, and icing separately. Rain and ice experiments document changes in propeller performance under their tested conditions. Use a wet-weather profile only for conditions supported by its measurements. Missing observations leave coverage unknown. [Carnegie Mellon flight measurements](https://theairlab.org/energy-dataset/) · [Iowa State rainfall and icing experiments](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dfd/2024/a30/9/)

**Battery temperature and usable energy.** Qualify usable Wh for the specific pack, starting charge, pack temperature, expected discharge load and cutoff voltage. For calibration, measure the energy delivered to the cutoff under a representative load. Internal resistance and voltage response vary with temperature and charge state. The caller supplies this qualified Wh before reserve deduction. Apply the reserve once. Compare observed peak current and voltage sag separately from the phase mean. [Experimental UAV Li-Po pack characterization](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/15/23/9249)

**Processor and cooling load.** Measure idle, perception, inference and simultaneous workloads for the installed Jetson, sensors and cooling system. Record duration in each workload, ambient and board temperature, fan state, and any clock throttling that extends the task. Jetson Orin Nano's VDD_IN measures module power. Its power-mode setting bounds operation, while a battery-side meter on the complete compute branch also captures the carrier, peripherals and converter losses supplied by that branch. The current that produces processor waste heat is already in the measured branch draw. A separately powered fan belongs to the branch that supplies it. [NVIDIA Orin power and thermal guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/archives/r36.4.4/DeveloperGuide/SD/PlatformPowerAndPerformance/JetsonOrinNanoSeriesJetsonOrinNxSeriesAndJetsonAgxOrinSeries.html)

## Calibrate and test a profile

1. Choose a configuration and a phase. Record flight IDs, equipment revision, payload, workload, phase definition, observed conditions and the meter boundary. Identify any unavailable channels. Use measured joint operating conditions to define a profile. Individually observed extremes may never have occurred together.
2. Repeat comparable runs. For example, to estimate the cost of inference during hover, vary the compute workload with payload, CG and weather held as closely as possible. Compare mean pack watts and convert the difference to Wh for a common duration. Report variation across runs. When several factors changed together, their combined effect is the supported comparison.
3. Reserve complete flights for evaluation before deriving profiles. For each held-out phase, record predicted and observed Wh and signed error (`predicted − observed`). Use measured durations to evaluate the power model. Evaluate duration forecasting separately using planned durations. Report the distribution of whole-flight errors, phase bias, missing evidence and sample count.
4. If a phase lacks matching evidence, collect comparable measurements or use an independently justified pack-power input. Keep the aircraft identity attached to each profile. The published Matrice 100 analysis uses takeoff, cruise and landing regimes. Its measurements support a reproducible comparison for that aircraft and phase convention. The X500 needs its own calibration. [Original data](https://doi.org/10.1184/R1/12683453) · [Authors' phase analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9403403/)
